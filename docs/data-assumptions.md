# Synthetic data assumptions

- The user-selected illustrative facility-map thresholds are Critical for an existing stockout or 0–45 days, Watch for 46–52 days, Later (labelled Stable before 2026-11-09) when the first stockout is after day 52 or there is none in the run, and Unknown when a facility/commodity has no usable run or forecast. These cutoffs were tuned on 2026-11-08 to make this synthetic scenario readable (county generator version 5, seed 42), are relative to the 90-day simulation horizon, and are not a standard of any kind, clinical or operational. They were chosen from real version 5 runs as the first candidate giving, at 0/20/40/100% shock, 2/0/38, 2/5/33, 10/18/12 and 40/0/0 Critical/Watch/Stable facilities; earlier cutoffs were 0–7/8–30 and, briefly, 0–45/46–75. Facility status uses the worst tracer; Unknown takes precedence over Stable when tracer data is missing. A run shorter than 52 days without a stockout cannot establish the Stable category and is treated as Unknown. Markers must disclose the driving tracer and days when available. The two Critical facilities at 0% are the planted pre-shock shortages (SYN-KE-042-F001 opens with 0 units of SYN-C01; SYN-KE-042-F003 runs out of SYN-C01 on day 6 without any shock) and are labeled “Already short before the shock”. The thresholds apply to each facility's first stockout day in the 90-day run and are fixed for the whole run; they do not change with the selected timeline day.

- County generator version 3 adds synthetic WGS84 latitude and longitude to the 40 invented Kisumu facilities. Seven invented cluster centers are placed in a roughly Kisumu-area box (approximately 0.25°S–0.05°S, 34.69°E–34.95°E), with seeded offsets of at most 0.003° on each axis. The centers are artificial grouping devices, not observed sub-county centroids, and are not verified against administrative boundaries or land/water. The planted donor and recipient share an offset and their invented centers are 0.108° apart in longitude, yielding approximately 12 km great-circle distance, consistent with the synthetic route assumption. Coordinates may fall outside actual sub-counties or on water and must never be interpreted as real facility locations. Every generated facility has `coordinates_synthetic: true`; snapshots from versions 1 and 2 remain unchanged and do not acquire coordinates.
- County generator version 4 preserves the invented coordinates and moves the planted synthetic near-expiry transfer to F002 → F001 for tracer C02. It adds 600 invented opening units at the donor, dispatches 100 units on day 45 and assumes arrival on day 46, expiry on day 90 and a route distance rounded from the synthetic points. In the seed-42 40% run, the recipient's first shock-caused event is F001/C02 on day 48. The 10-unit transfer and 50-unit emergency costs in the demo are illustrative assumptions (cost units, not prices). Versions 1–3 and their stored snapshots remain unchanged; version 3 retains its F002 → F003 C01 route.
- County generator version 5 (served by default; versions 1–4 unchanged) replaces the equal 30-day opening cover with invented cover per facility and commodity: each pair's lead time plus a seeded 4–40 extra days. In versions 1–4, 396 of 400 pairs had exactly 30 days of cover and every scheduled delivery was exactly 30 days of demand, so at a 40% logistics reduction 376 pairs ran out on day 48. With version 5 and seed 42, a 40% shock gives 209 shock-caused stockouts (211 in total, against 2 with no shock) spread from day 41 to day 89 across eight weeks, with at most 23 on one day. The planted chronic, neighbor and legacy donor records are kept, and the planted near-expiry transfer is regenerated for the first shock-caused stockout (seed 42: SYN-KE-042-F031, SYN-C04, day 41) from a donor in a neighboring sub-county with 600 invented extra units. These cover values are arbitrary assumptions, not observed stock levels. Version 5 also moves the seven invented cluster centers inland: in versions 3–4, 12 of the 40 seed-42 markers fell on Lake Victoria water in OpenStreetMap tiles. Every point of each version 5 center's jitter box (±0.003°) was checked against OpenStreetMap tiles at zoom 13 on 2026-11-08 and none is on mapped water; the seed-42 points were also checked in the rendered app map. Version 5 plants one transfer route for each of the four earliest 40% shock-caused stockouts, each from the nearest facility in a neighboring sub-county that holds 600 invented extra units of that commodity (seed 42: 11–24 km; first route SYN-KE-042-F029 to SYN-KE-042-F031). Every route distance is recomputed from the coordinates. Transfer and emergency costs (10 and 50 units) are illustrative cost units, not calibrated to real prices; the interface labels them “units (illustrative)” and keeps KES only for the funding trace. The locations remain invented and are labeled synthetic in the interface.

- County boundary review, 2026-11-07: [geoBoundaries gbOpen Kenya ADM1 metadata](https://www.geoboundaries.org/api/current/gbOpen/KEN/ADM1/) reports boundary ID `KEN-ADM1-32016919`, source `RCMRD GeoPortal, Africa GeoPortal`, represented year 2020, build date Dec 12, 2023, `boundaryLicense: Public Domain`, `licenseDetail: nan`, and `licenseSource: rcmrd.africageoportal.com/datasets/africageoportal`. The license source is a general portal and does not itself establish reuse terms for this specific geometry. Metadata was consulted on 2026-11-07; no boundary geometry was downloaded or included. Until the rights are confirmed, the national view uses an original schematic grid of official county names and codes, explicitly not to scale; real boundaries remain a future optional layer.

The generator produces synthetic demo records only. Model outputs remain illustrative and unvalidated.

- Phase 2b uses county names and codes from the [State Department for Devolution county directory](https://www.devolution.go.ke/county-information/) and Kisumu's seven sub-county names from the [Kisumu County Integrated Development Plan 2023–2027](https://www.kisumu.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/KISUMU-CIDP-III-2023-2027.pdf). These administrative names are real reference labels; facility records and all numerical planning values will be synthetic. No disease burden or donor dependence is inferred from these names.
- The Phase 2b planning generator uses FY 2024/25 national totals of KSh 601.69 billion in approved county budgets and KSh 469.34 billion in withdrawals authorized from county revenue funds to operational accounts, from the [Controller of Budget report hosted by Busia County](https://www.busiacounty.go.ke/assets/documents/uploads/CGBIRR%20August%202025.pdf), sections 2.2 and 2.3.2. Both totals cover **all sectors**, not medicines or donor logistics. County generator version 2 uses the report's county budget totals in table 2.5 as scale anchors, varies each weight by a seeded synthetic 95–105%, and renormalizes to the rounded national total. County release shares are independently invented; largest-remainder allocation preserves both national totals. Version 1 used invented county budget weights and remains available in existing immutable snapshots. No generated amount or release ratio is a real county figure, and the allocation is not a reconstruction of actual county budgets. County planning endpoints read only persisted, county-scoped snapshots.
- Kisumu pilot context: the [National Syndemic Diseases Control Council progress report](https://analytics.nsdcc.go.ke/estimates/PROGRESS-REPORT-2024.pdf) reports a 2023 estimate of 135,550 people living with HIV in Kisumu. The synthetic ARV tracer uses one percent of that county-wide estimate as an invented monthly panel scale, not an estimate of treatment coverage or consumption. The [Kenya Malaria Strategy](https://www.afro.who.int/sites/default/files/2025-03/Kenya%20Malaria%20Strategy%20%7C%202023-2027.pdf) includes Kisumu in its malaria endemicity classification; this supports including antimalarial tracers, not the planted stock levels. No claim about Kisumu donor dependence has been sourced; the 40% shock is a synthetic scenario assumption.
- The 40-facility Kisumu panel uses invented facility identities and assignments to the seven named sub-counties. It inherits the earlier national level-mix and lead-time proxies, not observed Kisumu facility distributions. All stock, consumption, individual routes, distances, funding amounts and dates are synthetic. In versions 1–3, three planted examples are `SYN-KE-042-F001` with zero opening stock and an invented six-month stockout history; `SYN-KE-042-F002` with a synthetic near-expiry surplus and a 12 km assumed route to at-risk `SYN-KE-042-F003`; and `SYN-042-FLOW-01` with an approved amount and no release as of the demo date. These are not real facilities or events. A county-scoped persistent run consumes the panel's opening stock and demonstrates the planted cases under the 40% shock. The single planted route and batch can be used in modeled mitigation ranking.

- Facility level mix is guided by the national public-facility counts in Table 2 of the [Kenya Health Labour Market Analysis 2023](https://files.aho.afro.who.int/afahobckpcontainer/production/files/Kenya_HLMA_Report_2023_v8.pdf), which cites the Kenya Master Facility List for 2022. It lists 4,807 level 2, 1,123 level 3, 360 level 4, and 14 level 5 public facilities. The 40-facility demo uses 29, 7, 3, and 1 respectively; rare higher levels are deliberately oversampled. It is a synthetic county and is not an estimate for any actual county.
- Generator version 2 anchors order turnaround to the FY 2024/25 achieved national primary-health-facility and hospital values in the Kenya National Treasury's [draft Health Sector Report](https://www.treasury.go.ke/sites/default/files/NEW/Draft-Health-Sector-Report-11.11.2025.pdf), table on page 80: 24.2 and 19.5 days respectively. Synthetic levels 2–3 use a rounded 24-day center and levels 4–5 a rounded 20-day center. The invented uniform plus/minus-five-day variation, level mapping and rounding are modelling choices, not measured distributions or facility observations. Version 1 stored snapshots remain unchanged.
- Generator version 3 uses the [U.S. President's Malaria Initiative Kenya FY 2022 plan](https://www.pmi.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/FY-2022-Kenya-MOP.pdf), tables A-13 through A-15, as national projected 2023 need anchors for three synthetic tracers: `SYN-C01` (6,067,513 ACT treatment courses), `SYN-C02` (9,649,693 rapid tests), and `SYN-C03` (1,087,808 injectable artesunate vials). These are forecasts of national need, not measured consumption or facility deliveries. For a wholly hypothetical scale, the generator divides each national projection by the 6,304 public facilities in the facility-mix source, allocates the equivalent of 40 shares across the synthetic panel using invented level weights, repeats the annual baseline over two years, and applies invented seasonal and random variation. National malaria need includes services beyond this public-facility denominator, malaria risk varies geographically, and the report does not support this local allocation. These proxies must not be interpreted as estimates for a real county or facility. Older snapshots remain unchanged.
- Generator version 4 retains those three projected malaria proxies and adds `SYN-C10`, using the same plan's table A-17 projected 2023 need of 1,241,175 sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine treatment courses. It also uses six historical public-sector family-planning consumption means from Appendix C of a [University of Nairobi analysis](https://erepository.uonbi.ac.ke/server/api/core/bitstreams/a391e712-280b-408a-b12d-413483cd201f/content): `SYN-C04` combined oral contraceptive cycles (1,174,159 annually), `SYN-C05` progestin-only pill cycles (280,442), `SYN-C06` injectable vials (2,572,465), `SYN-C07` intrauterine devices (289,015), `SYN-C08` one-rod implants (299,720), and `SYN-C09` two-rod implants (165,415). The means are rounded from the appendix's 2014–2017 totals; another summary table in the same analysis reports different means. These old aggregate figures are neither current demand nor facility-level observations. The same equal-share scaling uses 2022 facility counts with older demand data; this is a hypothetical construction, not an estimate. Monthly panel totals follow invented seasonality, then are apportioned using invented facility-level weights with integer remainder allocation.
- Generator version 5 uses the FY 2024/25 national health-products procurement value in the [Treasury health-sector report](https://www.treasury.go.ke/sites/default/files/NEW/Draft-Health-Sector-Report-11.11.2025.pdf) as a **scale proxy** for the total synthetic approved budget. The report states KSh 31,138,078,092.02; the generator truncates to whole shillings, scales by 40/6,304, and distributes the resulting total among ten synthetic budget lines using random weights. National procurement value is not an approved county or facility budget, includes products outside the ten tracers, and does not justify a per-line allocation. Previous generator snapshots remain unchanged.
- Generator version 6 uses the national health sector's first-quarter exchequer issues as a share of revised net estimates, 14%, from the [Controller of Budget's first-quarter FY 2024/25 report](https://parliament.go.ke/sites/default/files/2025-02/Report%20of%20the%20National%20Government%20Budget%20Implementation%20Review%20Report%20First%20Quarter%20FY%202024%3A25.pdf), as a **proxy** for synthetic release amounts on day 90. The original ratio covers the whole health sector and is not a commodity-budget or county release rate; the proxy does not establish such a rate. A synthetic procurement amount of three-fifths of each release and delivery days 91–150 are invented choices, not findings from the report. The approved-budget scale and release ratio come from different aggregate concepts and are combined solely to create a synthetic example.
- Seasonality, line-level budget allocations, procurement fractions, and delivery timing are arbitrary reproducible assumptions, not calibrated observations or clinical data. They cannot support operational decisions.

- `dawa_api.ingestion.aggregate_adapters` defines a provenance-carrying source interface and an unconfigured implementation that raises an explicit error for these missing measures. It does not import real facility records or silently invent values. The generator does not yet consume this adapter.
- The default pseudorandom seed is 42 and can be changed with `python -m dawa_api.ingestion.synthetic --seed 42`. The generator CLI emits JSON; all identifiers are invented. `python -m dawa_api.ingestion.persist --seed 42 --country KE` stores an immutable, country-scoped snapshot in Postgres. Runs reference the seed and generator version and use the stored snapshot. No real records are ingested.
- The scenario model assumes 30 days of opening stock. Versions 1–3 convert the latest monthly quantity to an integer daily demand with a minimum of one. Version 4 instead uses fixed-point stock units so one monthly unit corresponds to one-thirtieth of a unit per day, including zero demand without an artificial minimum. Versions 1 and 2 schedule replenishment on day 28 and every 30 days thereafter. Versions 3 and 4 assume an order placed on day 1 arrives after the facility's synthetic lead time, followed by deliveries every 30 days; the order cadence and interpreting the national turnaround anchor this way are modelling choices. A logistics shock reduces each replenishment by the selected percentage; a cash-delay shock shifts each delivery by the selected number of days. These simplifying assumptions are not validated and omit real procurement and transport behavior.
- Simulation version 2 assigns the invented facilities in snapshot order to groups of eight labelled synthetic districts. These groups do not correspond to real administrative areas. The district/commodity stockout day is the earliest simulated facility stockout in that group. Earlier stored runs retain their original output; their results do not carry this version marker or district summary.
- The `ZZ` country adapter is an intentionally non-operational placeholder, not a real country or data source.
